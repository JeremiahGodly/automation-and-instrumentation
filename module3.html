Part 1 · Syllabus Theory (Simplified & Clear)

3.1 Cable Transmission: Analog vs Digital

💡 In Simple Terms:
  • Analog 4–20 mA Current Loop: Like a garden hose where the flow rate of water represents pressure. Because the same current flows through every point in a series wire loop, line resistance of long cables causes zero voltage-drop errors!
  • Digital Transmission: Instead of sending a continuous voltage or current, we send coded packets of 1s and 0s. As long as the receiver can distinguish a 1 from a 0, the measurement has $100\%$ perfect accuracy!

Why 4–20 mA Current Loops are the Industrial Standard

  1. Immune to Cable Resistance: Kirchoff's Current Law ensures the exact same loop current reaches the receiver whether the cable is $10\text{ metres}$ or $1000\text{ metres}$ long.
  2. Live Zero at 4 mA: If a wire breaks or a transmitter burns out, loop current drops to $0\text{ mA}$. The controller immediately detects a wire-break alarm ($0\text{ mA}$) vs a legitimate zero reading ($4\text{ mA}$).
  3. Two-Wire Transmitter Power: The baseline $4\text{ mA}$ carries enough electric power to run the sensor's internal microelectronics over the exact same two wires!
  4. Simple Conversion: A precision $250\,\Omega$ resistor at the controller converts $4\text{–}20\text{ mA}$ into a standard $1\text{ to }5\text{ V}$ input for an A/D converter ($V = I\cdot R$).

3.2 Fibre-Optic Data Transmission

💡 In Simple Terms: Transmitting data as pulses of light flashed through a microscopic glass strand. Light trapped inside the glass core bounces along via Total Internal Reflection. Since glass carries light rather than electricity, it is $100\%$ immune to electromagnetic noise, sparks, and lightning strikes! 
 Electrical       +---------------+     Light     +-------------------------+     Light     +---------------+     Electrical
 Signal In =====> | LED / Laser   |==============>| Core (n1) | Cladding(n2)|==============>| Photodiode    |=====> Signal Out
                  | Driver Source |               +-------------------------+               | PIN/APD + Amp |
                  +---------------+                  Total Internal Reflection              +---------------+

Optics Principles & Formulae

  • Core & Cladding: Central glass core has refractive index $n_1$; surrounding cladding has slightly lower index $n_2$ ($n_1 > n_2$).
  • Critical Angle ($\theta_c$): Light striking the boundary at angles greater than $\theta_c$ reflects completely back into the core: $$ \theta_c = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right) $$
  • Numerical Aperture ($\text{NA}$): Measures light-gathering capacity of the fibre: $$ \text{NA} = \sin\theta_a = \sqrt{n_1^2 - n_2^2} $$
  • Signal Attenuation ($\alpha$): Loss of optical power over distance $L$: $$ \alpha = \frac{10}{L}\,\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{P_{in}}{P_{out}}\right)\ \text{dB/km} $$

Industrial Merits: Complete immunity to high-voltage EMI and motor surges, zero spark risk in explosive petroleum plants, ultra-high bandwidth, and total electrical isolation.

3.3 Pneumatic Transmission

💡 In Simple Terms: Transmitting process measurements using compressed air pressure inside a small tube ($3\text{ to }15\text{ psi}$). A flapper moves towards or away from an air nozzle, changing the back-pressure of air. 
 Regulated Supply Air (20 psi)
            |
      [ Orifice Restrictor ]-----+-----> To Pneumatic Booster Relay =====> 3 - 15 psi Output Signal
                                 |
                             [ Nozzle ] <--- Flapper Gap Controlled by Sensor Bellows

Working & Characteristics

  • Standard Range: $3\text{ to }15\text{ psi}$ ($20\text{ to }100\text{ kPa}$ or $0.2\text{ to }1.0\text{ bar}$). $3\text{ psi} = 0\%$ reading, $15\text{ psi} = 100\%$ reading. The $3\text{ psi}$ is a live zero.
  • Flapper-Nozzle Mechanism: Clean air at $20\text{ psi}$ passes through a fixed restrictor. When the flapper moves closer to the nozzle, escaping air is restricted, and nozzle back-pressure rises rapidly. A pneumatic relay boosts airflow volume to drive long transmission tubing.
  • Merits: Inherently safe (no electrical sparks) in explosive hydrocarbon environments; simple, rugged, and connects directly to pneumatic valves.
  • Limitations: Transmission lag (signals travel at sound speed $\approx 330\text{ m/s}$; pneumatic time constant grows with distance squared: $\tau \propto L^2$), limited distance ($<200\text{ m}$), and requires clean, bone-dry air supply.

3.4 Process Control Networks, Fieldbus, and Profibus

💡 In Simple Terms: In older plants, every single sensor required its own pair of copper wires running all the way back to the control room (miles of cables!). Fieldbus acts like a shared highway (bus): dozens of smart instruments share a single cable, chatting digitally, sending diagnostics, and even calculating PID control right in the field!

3.5 Radio vs Wireless Communication

💡 In Simple Terms: "Radio" refers to traditional point-to-point RF links (like walkie-talkies or remote pipeline telemetry). "Wireless" is the broader umbrella of modern networked standards (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, WirelessHART) that include addressing, routing, encryption, and mesh networking.
ParameterRadio CommunicationWireless Network Communication
DefinitionPoint-to-point / point-to-multipoint transmission using dedicated RF modems.Networked communication over any wire-free channel (Wi-Fi, ZigBee, WirelessHART, cellular).
TopologyFixed Star or Point-to-Point.Self-healing Mesh, Cluster-Tree, Star.
Frequency & LicensingOften dedicated licensed VHF/UHF bands ($150\text{–}900\text{ MHz}$).Unlicensed ISM bands ($2.4\text{ GHz}, 5\text{ GHz}, 868/915\text{ MHz}$).
ProtocolProprietary or simple Modbus over radio.Standardised layered stacks (IEEE 802.11, 802.15.4, WirelessHART).
Primary UseLong-range telemetry (water reservoirs, oil pipelines across deserts).Plant-floor sensor networks, mobile tablet HMIs, rotating equipment monitoring.

3.6 Virtual Instrumentation (VI) and Graphical Programming

💡 In Simple Terms:
  • Traditional Instrument: A closed metal box (like an oscilloscope or multimeter) with knobs and a fixed screen made by a vendor. If you need a new feature, you must buy a new box.
  • Virtual Instrument: A standard PC + plug-in measurement hardware + software (LabVIEW). "The Software IS the Instrument!" You design the knobs, graphs, and math on your computer screen. Change the software, and your multimeter instantly becomes a spectrum analyser!

A Process Control Network is a specialised industrial communication network connecting sensors, PLCs, DCS controllers, HMIs, and actuators across a manufacturing facility.

Key Functions:

  1. Real-Time Data Collection: Gathers measurements from field sensors with guaranteed deterministic response time.
  2. Command Transmission: Sends control outputs and safety interlock signals to final control actuators.
  3. Operator Supervision: Feeds real-time variables, alarm states, and trend graphs to HMI workstations.
  4. Remote Diagnostics & Configuration: Enables smart instrument calibration and status checks over the digital bus.
  5. Cybersecurity & Isolation: Strictly isolates critical automation networks from corporate IT networks to prevent cyber attacks.

How does Profibus help in industrial data communication? / Compare Profibus and Fieldbus.

3 marks
May 2024 · Q6Model Paper Q5

Profibus (Process Field Bus) provides a standardised, high-speed open communication network (IEC 61158) that replaces expensive multi-conductor cabling with a single 2-wire serial bus.

  • Profibus-DP (Decentralised Peripherals): High speed (up to $12\text{ Mbps}$) over RS-485 for factory automation, linking PLCs to remote I/O racks, drives, and valves using master-slave polling.
  • Profibus-PA (Process Automation): Runs at $31.25\text{ kbps}$ with bus power over intrinsically safe media for hazardous process areas.

Comparison: Profibus excels in high-speed discrete manufacturing and drives using cyclic master-slave polling; Foundation Fieldbus H1 excels in process plants by executing distributed PID control blocks directly inside field devices.

 Data In =======> | LED / Laser   |========================>| Core (n1) | Cladding(n2)|==============>| PIN Photodiode|=====> Data Out
                  | Driver Source |                         +-------------------------+               | or APD + Amp  |
                  +---------------+                              OPTICAL FIBRE CABLE                  +---------------+
                    TRANSMITTER                               Total Internal Reflection                   RECEIVER

2. Detailed Functional Blocks

  1. Optical Transmitter: Converts electrical voltage pulses into optical light pulses.
    • LED (Light Emitting Diode): Inexpensive, rugged, wide spectral width, used for short-range multimode links ($850\text{–}1300\text{ nm}$).
    • Laser Diode (LD): High power, coherent narrow beam, fast switching ($>1\text{ Gbps}$), used for long-distance single-mode links ($1310\text{–}1550\text{ nm}$).
  2. Optical Fibre Cable: Consists of an inner glass core (refractive index $n_1$) surrounded by an outer glass cladding (refractive index $n_2$). Since $n_1 > n_2$, light entering within the acceptance angle experiences Total Internal Reflection (TIR): $$ \text{Critical Angle: } \theta_c = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right), \qquad \text{Numerical Aperture: } \text{NA} = \sqrt{n_1^2 - n_2^2} $$
  3. Optical Receiver: Detects incoming light photons and converts them back to electrical current via photoelectric effect using a PIN Photodiode or an Avalanche Photodiode (APD), followed by a transimpedance amplifier and pulse shaper.

3. Industrial Advantages & Disadvantages

  • Advantages: $100\%$ immune to electromagnetic interference (EMI), radio frequency interference (RFI), and high-voltage surges; no spark hazard in explosive oil/gas zones; enormous bandwidth; ultra-low signal attenuation ($0.2\text{ dB/km}$); galvanic isolation eliminates ground loops.
  • Disadvantages: Higher cost of electro-optical transceivers; fragile glass cores require careful installation; specialised fusion splicing equipment needed for repairs.

Q. Explain pneumatic data transmission with neat sketch.

8 marks
Jun 2023 · Q16(a)

Pneumatic transmission represents process measurements as proportional air pressures ($3\text{ to }15\text{ psi}$) conveyed through small-bore copper or nylon tubing.

  Supply Air 20 psi
        |
  [ Restrictor Orifice ]-----+-----> To Pneumatic Booster Relay =====> 3 - 15 psi Output Signal
                             |
                         [ Nozzle ] <==== Variable Gap x Controlled by Sensor (Flapper)

Working Principle (Flapper-Nozzle System)

  1. Clean, regulated dry air at $20\text{ psi}$ ($1.4\text{ bar}$) flows through a fixed restrictor orifice to an open nozzle.
  2. A movable metal leaf (the flapper), positioned by the primary sensor (bellows, Bourdon tube, or diaphragm), covers the nozzle opening.
  3. When measured variable increases: Flapper moves closer to the nozzle $\implies$ air escape is throttled $\implies$ back-pressure inside the nozzle chamber rapidly rises.
  4. When measured variable decreases: Flapper moves away $\implies$ air escapes freely $\implies$ back-pressure drops.
  5. A Pneumatic Booster Relay amplifies the small nozzle airflow volume to drive long field tubing with minimal lag. A feedback bellows provides negative feedback to make output pressure strictly linear with flapper motion.

Standard Calibration: $0\%\text{ process value} = 3\text{ psi}$ ($20\text{ kPa}$), $100\%\text{ process value} = 15\text{ psi}$ ($100\text{ kPa}$). Live zero of $3\text{ psi}$ detects air line leaks.

2. Detailed Description of Each Block

  1. Sensors & Transducers: Detect the physical process variable (e.g., thermocouple for temperature, strain gauge for force) and produce raw electrical signals.
  2. Signal Conditioning Module: Amplifies microvolt signals, strips high-frequency noise via anti-aliasing filters, provides galvanic isolation, and supplies bridge excitation.
  3. Data Acquisition (DAQ) Hardware: Plug-in card (PCIe, USB, or PXI module) containing:
    • Multiplexer (MUX): Allows multiple sensor channels to share one ADC.
    • Sample-and-Hold (S/H): Freezes the analog voltage during conversion.
    • Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC): Converts analog voltage into $n$-bit digital numbers ($\text{Resolution} = V_{FS}/2^n$). Sampling rate must satisfy Nyquist criterion: $f_s \ge 2 f_{max}$.
    • DAC & Digital I/O: Generates analog output voltages and digital on/off signals for control.
  4. Computer & Driver Software: Standard PC running driver software (e.g., NI-DAQmx) that abstracts hardware into standard software function calls.
  5. Application Software (LabVIEW): The core of the virtual instrument:
    • Front Panel: The user interface mimicking a physical instrument panel with knobs, sliders, LED switches, and digital graphs.
    • Block Diagram: The graphical source code where functional icons are wired together following the dataflow model.

1. Core Concepts of Graphical Programming (LabVIEW "G")

  • Dataflow Execution: Execution is governed entirely by the movement of data, not sequential line numbers. A node executes if and only if data is available at all its input terminals!
  • Dual-Window Concept:
    • Front Panel: Represents the user interface with Controls (inputs) and Indicators (outputs).
    • Block Diagram: Contains graphical nodes (functions, subVIs, loops) connected by colour-coded wires carrying data.
  • Automatic Parallelism: If two wires branch to independent function blocks, both blocks execute simultaneously in separate threads on multi-core processors without any complex programming!
